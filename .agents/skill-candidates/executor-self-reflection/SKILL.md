# Executor self-reflection

Review the accepted assignment from the executor's perspective. Your output is input to a separate lead reflection; the real lead decides and assigns changes.

Use the supplied task, source-session and accepted-result references to establish which work is being reviewed. The fork's earlier implementation instructions describe that work; they are not a request to continue it. Stay within the reflection mandate. Do not change code, guidance or board records, dispatch agents, or launch another reflection.

## Find the useful lesson

Examine the original brief, your decisions, actual lead corrections and the accepted result. Look for cheaper future changes and maintenance as well as a better path to that result:

- What necessary input or decision was missing, ambiguous or contradictory?
- Which existing requirement or applicable practice did you miss?
- Which investigation, retry, check or handoff consumed effort without helping?
- What could the lead have framed earlier, and what could you have resolved independently?

These are prompts for judgment, not required sections or a quota of findings. A changed user requirement is not automatically your mistake. A lead's criticism is not automatically correct. Conversely, a clear existing requirement is not a briefing defect merely because you overlooked it.

Use the available conversation first. Read the relevant assignment, exact submitted revision, correction or check only when it can resolve a concrete question. A reused checkout may no longer contain the reviewed work. Preserve missing context and uncertainty; do not decode opaque reasoning or reconstruct unavailable history.

Prefer removing an unnecessary step, supplying an input earlier, or fixing an interface/check over adding standing instructions. Judge concrete lasting benefit against implementation, recurring complexity and context burden. Account for the full accepted-result token cost: added guidance can repay itself by preventing rework. Savings are not mandatory; explain how any increased full spending would repay, in which observable measures and over what bounded relevant use. Do not present a forecast as demonstrated payback or treat examples as a closed checklist. One clearly attributable defect can support a bounded correction without repeated incidents. Report a concrete potential release incident with its exposure and consequences to the real lead; do not wait for it to occur or decide the release yourself. Keep forecasts distinct from observed effects.

## Return a compact result

For each useful proposal, explain the observed episode and source reference, likely cause, smallest change, appropriate owner, relevant next use and a proportionate check. Distinguish measured waste from predicted savings. Include supported criticism of the brief and of your own work; do not force equal blame or agreement.

Return only findings worth the real lead's attention, plus any material uncertainty. If none qualify, say that no useful improvement was found and finish. Respect the supplied investigation allowance; a missing decisive fact can be an inconclusive result rather than a larger investigation.

Do not create tasks or propose changes merely to justify reflection. The launcher retains and delivers your final result; an extra notification or self-started lead session would duplicate that responsibility.
